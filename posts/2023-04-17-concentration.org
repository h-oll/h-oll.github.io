1. Definitions

#+begin_html
  </h2>
#+end_html

<<text-1>>

<<org1f2f8ba>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

The Lipschitz constant for (H) a self-adjoint operator is defined by:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\|H\|_L = 2 \max_{v\in V} \min_{H_{\setminus v}} \|H - H_{\setminus v}\otimes \mathbb{I}_v\|_{\infty}
\end{equation}

<<orgd654d67>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

The sandwiched Renyi divergence of order (\alpha \in (1,+\infty)) is
defined for two quantum states (\rho, \sigma) with
(\mathrm{supp} \rho \subset \mathrm{supp} \sigma) as

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
D_{\alpha} (\rho \| \sigma) = \frac{1}{\alpha-1} \log \mathrm{tr}\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \rho \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right )^\alpha\right]
\end{equation}

<<org3661770>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

A state (\sigma) on (V) qudits satisfies a Gaussian concentration
inequality of parameter (c) if there is a constant (K) such that for any
(a) and any observable (O):

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\Pr_{\sigma} (|O - \langle O\rangle_\sigma \mathbb{I}| > a |V|) \leq K \exp \left( - \frac{c a^2 |V|}{\| \sigma^{-1/2} O \sigma^{1/2}\|_L^2}\right)
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Above (|A| = \sqrt {A^\dagger A}), and (\Pr/\sigma(|O-\langle
O\rangle/\sigma \mathbb{I} > a|V|)) means that for (E) the positive part
of (|O-\langle O\rangle_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}|-a|V|\mathbb{I}), we have
(\mathrm{tr}(E\sigma)) bounded by the right hand side of the equation.
Note that in the cases where (\sigma) and (O) commute (| \sigma^{-1/2} O
\sigma^{1/2}|_L^2) reduces to (| O|_L^2).

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

2. Transferring inequalities

#+begin_html
  </h2>
#+end_html

<<text-2>>

<<orgbdcac30>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Let (\rho, \sigma) be two quantum states on (\mathcal H_V). Then for any
POVM element (0 \leq E \leq \mathbb{I}) and (\alpha >1),

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}[E\rho] \leq \exp\left( \frac{\alpha -1}{\alpha} (D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma) + \log(\mathrm{tr}[E\sigma]))\right)
\end{equation}

<<org7e0e1fd>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Using the circularity of the trace we have:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{align}
\mathrm{tr}[E\rho]
& = \mathrm{tr}[\sigma^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} E \sigma^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \times \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \rho  \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}] \\
& \leq \left\{\mathrm{tr}\left[ \left( \sigma^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} E \sigma^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^\beta \right ]\right\}^{\frac{1}{\beta}} \times \left\{\mathrm{tr}\left[ \left( \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \rho \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \right)^\alpha \right ]\right\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
\end{align}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

where the last line is obtained by applying H"older inequality, which
holds for (\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = 1).

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Using the Araki-Lieb-Thirring inequality followed by (E \leq \mathbb{I})
we obtain:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{align}
\mathrm{tr}\left( \sigma^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} E \sigma^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^\beta
& \leq \mathrm{tr}\left( \sigma^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)\beta}{2\alpha}} E^\beta \sigma^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)\beta}{2\alpha}}\right) \\
& \leq \mathrm{tr}\left( \sigma^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)\beta}{2\alpha}} E \sigma^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)\beta}{2\alpha}}\right).
\end{align}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

As (\beta = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha -1}) we have

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}\left( \sigma^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)\beta}{2\alpha}} E \sigma^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)\beta}{2\alpha}}\right)=\mathrm{tr}(E\sigma).
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Additionally, note that

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\left \{ \mathrm{tr}\left[ \left( \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \rho  \sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}} \right)^\alpha \right ]\right\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}} = \exp (\frac{\alpha -1}{\alpha} D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma)).
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Combining the two gives:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{align}
\mathrm{tr}[E\rho]
& \leq (tr[E\sigma]) \times \exp\left( \frac{\alpha -1}{\alpha} (D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma))\right) \\
& \leq \exp\left( \frac{\alpha -1}{\alpha} (D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma))  \log (\mathrm{tr}[E\sigma])\right).
\end{align}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Remark that the theorem bounds the probability of observing (E) on
(\rho) based on the product of the probability of observing (E) on
(\sigma) times a quantity that depends on (D_\alpha(\rho|\sigma)), which
can be seen as a measure of the distance between the two states (\rho)
and (\sigma). This will become interesting whenever
(D_\alpha(\rho|\sigma)) becomes small.

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

<<org4695ae0>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

If (\sigma) satisfies a Gaussian concentration inequality

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\Pr_{\sigma} (|O - \langle O\rangle_\sigma \mathbb{I}| > a |V|) \leq K \exp \left( - \frac{c a^2 |V|}{\| \sigma^{-1/2} O \sigma^{1/2}\|_L^2}\right)
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

for some constant (c, K > 0), then for any (\alpha > 1):

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\Pr_{\rho} (|O - \langle O\rangle_\sigma \mathbb{I}| > a |V|) \leq \exp  \left(\frac{\alpha -1}{\alpha}\left(D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma) - \frac{c a^2 |V|}{\| \sigma^{-1/2} O \sigma^{1/2}\|_L^2} + \log(K)\right)\right)
\end{equation}

<<org57fda40>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

For an input state (\rho), a noisy circuit evolution (\mathcal{N}(\rho))
and a state (\sigma) satisfying a Gaussian concentration inequality,
whenever there is a value of (\alpha) and (a) such that

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\frac{D_{\alpha}(\mathcal N(\rho) \| \sigma)}{|V|} < \frac{c a^2}{\| \sigma^{-1/2} O \sigma^{1/2}\|_L^2} - \frac{\log(K)}{|V|},
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

then the probability of observing an outcome of (O) outside the interval
(\langle O \rangle_{\sigma} \pm a|V|) decreases exponentially with
(|V|).

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

3. Bounding (D_{\alpha}(\mathcal N(\rho) | \sigma))

#+begin_html
  </h2>
#+end_html

<<text-3>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Such bounds can be obtained for noisy circuits where each layer (i) is
followed by a noisy channel (\mathcal N) with a fixed point (\sigma)
satisfying a strong data processing inequality:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
D_\alpha(\mathcal N(\rho) \| \sigma)\leq (1-q) D_\alpha (\rho \|\sigma), \ \forall \rho.
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

In such cases we obtain

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

<<org7432983>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Let (\mathcal N) be a quantum channel with a unique fixed point (\sigma)
and satisfying a strong data processing inequality for some (\alpha >
1), then for any other channels ({\Phi/i}/{i\leq l}) we have:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
D_\alpha\left( \mathcal P (\rho) \| \sigma \right) \leq (1-q)^l D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma) + \sum_{i\leq l} (1-q)^{l-i} D_\infty (\Phi_i(\sigma) \| \sigma)
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

where (D_\infty(\rho | \sigma) = \log | \sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}
\rho \sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}|/\infty), and (\mathcal P = \prod/{i \leq l}
\Phi_i \circ \mathcal N).

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

<<org4062ecd>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

We proceed by induction on (l). For (l=1), we use the data processed
triangle inequality:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
D_\alpha(\Phi_1 \circ \mathcal N  (\rho) \|\sigma) \leq D_\alpha(\mathcal N (\rho) \| \sigma) + D_\infty (\Phi_1 (\sigma) \| \sigma) \leq (1-q) D_\alpha(\rho \| \sigma) + D_\infty (\Phi_1 (\sigma) \| \sigma).
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

The induction is performed in the same way, assuming the property holds
for some (l) and then applying the data processed triangle inequality.

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

Note that for unital channels (\mathcal N(\sigma) = \sigma) as the fixed
point of the channel is (\mathbb{I}). This implies that (D_\alpha\left(
\mathcal P (\rho) | \sigma \right)) will always converge to 0.

  </p>

4.2. Relative entropy and Pinsker inequality

#+begin_html
  </h3>
#+end_html

<<text-4-2>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

The Pinsker inequality relates the relative entropy to the trace
distance. As such it allows to bound the trace distance using
information theoretic arguments:

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
D_1(\rho\|\sigma) \geq \frac{1}{2\log 2} \| \rho -\sigma \|_{\mathrm{tr}}^2
\end{equation}

4.3. Hölder inequality

#+begin_html
  </h3>
#+end_html

<<text-4-3>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

For (A), (B) operators on (\mathcal H)

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\|AB\|_1 \leq \|A\|_p \|B\|_q, \quad \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1
\end{equation}

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html

which translates for matrices into

#+begin_html
  </p>
#+end_html

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(|AB|) \leq [\mathrm{tr}(|A|^{p})]^{1/p} \times [\mathrm{tr}(|B|^{q})]^{1/q}, \quad \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1.
\end{equation}

4.4. Araki-Lieb-Thirring inequality

#+begin_html
  </h3>
#+end_html

<<text-4-4>>

#+begin_html
  <p>
#+end_html
